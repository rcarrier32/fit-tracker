# Daily mobility, joint-health and "durability" routines from basketball-centric coaches

Research context: November 2026. Direct page fetches were blocked by the session network for every coach site tried (pjfperformance.com, kneesovertoesguy.substack.com, thebasketballdoctors.com, youtube.com, recapio.com all failed DNS resolution). Everything below comes from web-search result snippets and summaries, not from reading full pages, videos or transcripts. Treat exact doses as "as reported in search snippets" and verify against the primary page before building them into the app.

## 1. PJF Performance / Paul Fabritz: what he recommends for durability, and in what doses

### Takeaway
What I could document from PJF is mostly program-level: the Durability Code (tendon-tolerance plyometrics), one short foot/Achilles "springs" drill dosed at 3-4 x 20 s per leg, 3-5 days/week, a balance/ankle progression video, and podcast themes (daily "movement snacks" every hour, 10k steps, tendon changes after 30, "athletic aging"). He has publicly moved away from early tib-raise hype. I found no published PJF "daily 5-10 minute routine" with exercise-by-exercise doses.

### Cited Findings
- **Durability Code** is a progressive plyometric program aimed at tendon tolerance. It is positioned against the leading causes of tendinopathy in jumpers ("Jumper's Knee, Achilles Tendinopathy, Plantar Fasciopathy") and is built to handle the high ground-reaction forces of jumping, running and cutting. — [PJF: Durability Code](https://www.pjfperformance.com/the-durability-code/) (seen via search snippet; page itself not reachable)
- **"Build Your Springs" Achilles/foot strength, elasticity and stability drill**: 3-4 sets of 20 seconds per leg, 3-5 days per week, paired with single-leg hops. — [PJF: Build Your Springs](https://www.pjfperformance.com/build-your-springs-achilles-foot-strength-elasticity-and-stability-drill/) (search snippet)
- **Balance and ankle progression** video, framed as "Stop Spraining Ankles": a progression for balance and building the tissues around the ankle. Exercise list and doses not retrievable. — [YouTube: @PJFPerformance Balance & Ankle Progression](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ah3HxxuovPQ)
- **Mac McClung Jump Program** (16 weeks) includes "injury reduction exercises to strengthen your knees". — [PJF: Mac McClung Jump Program](https://www.pjfperformance.com/mac-mcclung-jump-program/)
- **Extensive (knee-dominant) jumps** are credited to Fabritz: the aim is priming the knee for explosive movement at deep knee angles, keeping the focus on the knees rather than over-pointing the ankles. Fabritz reportedly recommends 3 x 12. — [A1 Athlete: exercises to increase vertical jump](https://a1athlete.com/exercises-to-increase-vertical-jump/) (secondary source)
- **PJF Podcast ep. 058, "Knee Health Myths, Truth About Tib Raises, Stretching and More"**, per third-party summaries:
  - early claims that tib raises improve blood flow to the knee and slow triple flexion "have been debunked";
  - avoiding heel strike is overrated, because heel contact is a natural part of deceleration;
  - stretching won't heal knee pain, but is also unlikely to cause it;
  - the episode also covers deep range-of-motion training and icing;
  - it includes a BPC-157 anecdote (30 days oral, seemed to reduce inflammation, didn't heal the pain).
  — [Player.fm ep. 058](https://uk.player.fm/series/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance/ep-058-knee-health-myths-truth-about-tib-raises-stretching-and-more); [Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/058-knee-health-myths-truth-about-tib-raises-stretching-and-more-pjf-podcast-by-pjfperformance) (summary only)
- **PJF Podcast ep. 050, "10 Daily Habits That Will Level You Up Mentally and Physically"** (11 July 2023, ~40 min). Timecodes include Movement Snacks, Morning Sunlight, no phone in the first hour, Walk 10,000 Steps, Breath work and protein intake. His X posts echo it: movement routines every hour, and a 10k-step walk as a "cheat code" for better sleep. — [Apple Podcasts: PJF Podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/nz/podcast/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance/id1462156813); [Paul Fabritz on X](https://x.com/pjfperformance?lang=en)
- **Age-related episodes:**
  - ep. 054 has a segment "What happens after Age 30 to tendons";
  - ep. 055 is "Do Athletes Need Direct Core Training + The Keys to Athletic Aging" (3 May 2024).
  — [Apple Podcasts listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/nz/podcast/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance/id1462156813); [Listen Notes](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance-GA9LlOs-bS8/)
- Fabritz is described as an NBA strength and conditioning coach; the podcast is his official show. — [iHeart: PJF Podcast](https://www.iheart.com/podcast/256-the-pjf-podcast-elite-spor-43070781/)

### Inferences
- PJF's durability model leans on **tendon loading** (isometrics/plyometrics such as the "springs" drill and extensive jumps) and **frequent low-dose movement** (hourly movement snacks, daily walking), not on static stretching or tib raises as magic fixes. A routine "in the PJF spirit" for a 40-year-old would probably include a short foot/ankle elastic drill (e.g. 3 x 20 s per leg) most days and keep tib raises as general strength, not knee-pain cures.
- The "3-5 days/week" dose on the springs drill suggests PJF does not prescribe tendon-loading plyos strictly daily. Daily work would be lower-intensity balance and mobility.

### Gaps
- I could not get the actual exercise list, sets/reps or weekly layout of the Durability Code, the balance/ankle progression, or any PJF hooper warm-up. Every source page and YouTube was network-blocked.
- I found no published PJF "5-minute/10-minute daily routine" and no PJF soleus-isometric dose. Search for "Fabritz soleus isometric" returned nothing PJF-authored.
- I don't know the content of ep. 054 (tendons after 30) or ep. 055 (athletic aging) beyond their titles. Those are the most relevant sources for a 40-year-old and should be listened to directly.

## 4. Ben Patrick (Knees Over Toes / ATG): doses for the knee and ankle staples

### Takeaway
Patrick is the most specific source on doses. Tibialis raises run about 2 x up to 15, or at least 15 reps at around 20% of bodyweight on a tib bar. ATG split squat runs from 4 x 8 up to 8 x 8 depending on program. Backward walking is the entry point ("a few minutes a day"). He programs by session or week (e.g. full body twice a week), not as a single daily routine. He has basketball roots (training young players with knee pain in Clearwater, FL).

### Cited Findings
- Knee Ability Pro includes Tibialis Raise 2 x up to 15 and, on the Thursday session, ATG Split Squat 8 x 8. — [Ben's Substack: Knee Ability Zero, Basics & Pro](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/knee-ability-zero-basics-and-pro); [Knee Ability Series](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-knee-ability-series-3-of-10)
- The minimalist full-body program lists ATG Split Squat 4 x 8. In his own full-body routine he describes 4 sets of up to 12 per side. — [ATG Minimalistic Full-Body Program](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-minimalistic-full-body-program); [Full Body ATG Twice Per Week](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/full-body-atg-twice-per-week); [ATG Athletic Routine](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-athletic-routine)
- Patrick reportedly recommends at least 15 reps of tib-bar raises at 20% of bodyweight as a robustness benchmark. — [BarBend: 7 exercises to prevent knee injuries](https://barbend.com/kneesovertoesguy-7-exercises-to-help-prevent-knee-injuries/)
- A secondary summary lists ATG Zero tib raise at 25 reps. — [Scribd: ATG Zero Program](https://www.scribd.com/document/869788949/Atg-Zero-Program) (unofficial)
- One secondary source says 5 x 4-6 for the split squat; that conflicts with his own posts. — [A1 Athlete: ATG split squat](https://a1athlete.com/atg-split-squat/)
- **Backward walking / reverse sled:** described as a starting point; beginners can do a few minutes a day and build up. He says backward walking alone is solid for his many elderly clients but not enough for demanding basketball. Reverse sled is a foundation (an idea credited to Louie Simmons). — [SetForSet: Rogan x Patrick on knee pain](https://www.setforset.com/blogs/news/joe-rogan-kneesovertoesguy-ben-patrick-how-to-fix-knee-pain); [Just Fly Sports podcast 148](https://www.just-fly-sports.com/podcast-148-ben-patrick/)
- **Basketball roots:** knee pain from age 12 and multiple knee surgeries led to the method; he then ran a gym training young basketball players with knee pain in Clearwater. On the Tim Ferriss Show (ep. #835, Nov 2025) he discussed "20-minute workouts" and going "from chronic knee pain to dunking basketballs". — [Morning Brew](https://www.morningbrew.com/stories/2022/08/18/the-humble-empire-of-knees-over-toes-guy); [Shortform summary of Tim Ferriss #835](https://www.shortform.com/podcast/episode/the-tim-ferriss-show-2025-11-12-episode-summary-835-ben-patrick-kneesovertoesguy-20-minute-workouts-that-produce-wild-results-from-chronic-knee-pain-to-dunking-basketballs-lessons-from-charles-poliquin-bulletproofing-the-lower-body-and-more); [Wikipedia: Ben Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Patrick)

### Inferences
- For a 40-year-old, Patrick-style work would sit naturally as:
  - a few minutes of backward walking most days;
  - tib raises (about 2 x 15-25);
  - ATG split squat / knee-over-toe lunges at light load, a few sets of 8.
- Heavier ATG loading would be 2-3x/week rather than daily, matching his "twice per week" full-body structure.
- PJF's ep. 058 view (tib raises don't improve knee blood flow) and Patrick's emphasis on tib raises don't fully conflict. Both keep tib raises as strength/robustness work; PJF disputes the mechanism claims.

### Gaps
- I couldn't find a Patrick routine specifically for players over 40. Couch stretch, 90/90, Copenhagen and "Elephant walk" doses from his programs couldn't be retrieved because the Substack fetch was blocked.

## 5. Alan Stein Jr., The Basketball Doctors, and other basketball warm-up voices

### Takeaway
Alan Stein's published framework orders the session as **pre-hab, then a standardized dynamic warm-up, then dynamic flexibility**, using floor mobility and ankle work such as toe walks ("great for ankles and Achilles"). His drill lists are mostly in paid DVD/video products. The best evidence-backed basketball item is a roughly 10-minute neuromuscular warm-up linked to about 36% fewer knee/ankle injuries in youth basketball.

### Cited Findings
- Stein's DeMatha warm-up/flexibility DVD contains 50 warm-up exercises for practice, workout or game prep, focused on dynamic flexibility for mobility and range of motion. — [Hoop Scoop: Alan Stein's DeMatha Warm-Up & Flexibility](https://www.mensbasketballhoopscoop.com/alan-steins-dematha-basketball-warm-up-flexibility-basketball-coaching-dvd/)
- Stein's sequencing: "After going through pre-hab exercises, it is time to get into your standardized warm-up", and "After your players are warmed up, it is time to go into dynamic flexibility." — [PlaySports TV: warm-up drills](https://www.playsportstv.com/basketball/warm-ups-drills); [PlaySports TV: dynamic flexibility](https://www.playsportstv.com/basketball/dynamic-flexibility)
- His Better Basketball series groups drills into Pre Hab, Warmup, Mobility and Balance. — [Better Basketball: Alan Stein's Greatest Hits](https://betterbasketball.com/alan-steins-greatest-hits/)
- Unofficial notes of his "Active Warm-Up & Core Training" list toe walks and similar ankle moves ("great for ankles and Achilles"). — [Scribd notes](https://es.scribd.com/document/94292102/Alan-Stein-Active-Warmup-and-Core-Training) (unofficial)
- The NBPA warm-up blog recommends combining dynamic and static stretching, with knee hugs, lunge-and-twist reaches and lateral runs with knee lifts. It says the lunge-and-twist helps knee stability. — [NBPA: Keys to a Proper Warm Up](https://nbpa.com/grassroots/blog/stretching-tips-keys-to-a-proper-warm-up)
- A roughly 10-minute neuromuscular warm-up reduced ankle and knee injuries by 36% in youth basketball. — [3CB Performance Substack](https://3cbperformance.substack.com/p/this-10-minute-warm-up-reduced-knee) (secondary write-up of a study; the original study wasn't retrieved)

### Gaps

## 6. Veteran NBA players (35+) and what they publicly describe

### Takeaway
Steve Nash is the only veteran with a documented, specific daily routine: Block Training's **"Daily Eight"**, eight minutes of low-intensity mobility, stability, balance and coordination work done daily. Chris Paul and Vince Carter coverage stresses training detail, body awareness and diet, not named drills.

### Cited Findings
- **Steve Nash** co-founded the Block Training app. Its "Daily Eight" is eight minutes of low-intensity movement and stability work that improves and maintains joint mobility, stability, balance and coordination, built as a "daily, repeatable habit". He frames longevity around healthspan. — [Health Insight: Steve Nash & Block Training](https://www.healthinsight.ca/wellness/men-health-care/steve-nashs-insights-on-the-vitality-of-physical-and-mental-health-introducing-block-training-his-groundbreaking-training-app/); [Scottsdale Independent](https://www.yourvalley.net/scottsdale-independent/stories/steve-nash-on-how-to-live-the-good-life-longer,513735)

## Cross-source synthesis: recurring exercises, frequency, older players, short routines

### Takeaway
Across the sources I could reach, the recurring themes are:
1. **Foot, ankle and Achilles elastic/balance work**: PJF springs drill and balance progression; Stein's toe walks.
2. **Tibialis and backward walking**: Patrick.
3. **Deep knee-flexion strength**: ATG split squat; PJF extensive jumps and deep-ROM training.
4. **Daily low-dose movement**: PJF movement snacks and 10k steps; Nash's 8-minute Daily Eight.
5. **Dynamic, not static, pre-court warm-ups**: Stein; the NBPA blog; the 10-minute neuromuscular warm-up evidence.

The pattern is light daily mobility and balance, with tendon-loading plyos and loaded ATG work 2-5x/week.

### Cited Findings
- Daily, under 10 minutes: Nash's Daily Eight — [Health Insight](https://www.healthinsight.ca/wellness/men-health-care/steve-nashs-insights-on-the-vitality-of-physical-and-mental-health-introducing-block-training-his-groundbreaking-training-app/); PJF's hourly movement routines — [PJF on X](https://x.com/pjfperformance?lang=en)
- 3-5 days/week: PJF's foot/Achilles springs drill, 3-4 x 20 s per leg — [PJF](https://www.pjfperformance.com/build-your-springs-achilles-foot-strength-elasticity-and-stability-drill/)
- About 2x/week loaded: Patrick's full-body ATG — [Substack](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/full-body-atg-twice-per-week)
- Pre-court: pre-hab, then dynamic warm-up, then dynamic flexibility (Stein) — [PlaySports TV](https://www.playsportstv.com/basketball/warm-ups-drills); about 10 minutes of neuromuscular warm-up — [3CB](https://3cbperformance.substack.com/p/this-10-minute-warm-up-reduced-knee)
- Age specifics: tendon changes after 30 and "athletic aging" are PJF podcast topics (content not retrieved) — [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/nz/podcast/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance/id1462156813). Patrick says backward walking suits elderly clients but isn't enough for basketball — [SetForSet](https://www.setforset.com/blogs/news/joe-rogan-kneesovertoesguy-ben-patrick-how-to-fix-knee-pain)

### Inferences
- A defensible 5-7 item daily routine drawn from these sources:
  1. backward walking, 2-3 min (Patrick);
  2. tib raises, 1-2 x 15-25 (Patrick);
  3. single-leg balance / foot "springs" drill, 2-3 x 20 s per leg (PJF);
  4. light ATG split squat or knee-over-toe lunge, 1-2 x 8 per side (Patrick);
  5. a dynamic hip/T-spine lunge-and-twist (NBPA/Stein);
  6. toe walks / ankle work (Stein).
- Plyometric "Durability Code"-style work and heavier ATG loading would go 2-4x/week on court days rather than daily.
- Several staples the user listed were **not documented in any source I could reach**: couch stretch, 90/90, Copenhagen, ankle CARs, pogo hops, deep squat hold, dead hangs, band shoulder work. Their inclusion would rest on other researchers' sources or general practice.

### Gaps
- Nothing shoulder- or spine-specific was documented from any basketball-centric coach in this pass.
- Nothing I found addressed coaches who are on their feet demonstrating all day.
- Exact PJF doses beyond the springs drill, and any PJF "older hooper" protocol, are undocumented here because the network blocked pjfperformance.com and youtube.com. Allowing those hosts (and kneesovertoesguy.substack.com, thebasketballdoctors.com) would let a follow-up pass fill these gaps.
