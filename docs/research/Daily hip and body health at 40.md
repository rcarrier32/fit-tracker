# Ten minutes a day, the HoopStudy way

The best daily routine for an almost-40 coach who sits at a computer is a **roughly 12-minute follow-along flow built on DJ Sackmann's own HoopStudy "Ten Minute Routine"**, alternating between A and B days. It has three parts. A short standing warm-up loads the ankles and calves (pogos, calf raises, ankle circles, arm circles). A floor block covers core, breathing and hip rotation (dead bugs, 90/90 hold and transitions, bridges). A lunge-and-squat block covers hip extension, T-spine and deep flexion (Spider-Man/World's Greatest Stretch, a couch or half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch, deep squat with overhead reach). It finishes with what Sackmann's routine lacks and the desk evidence supports most: **band neck/shoulder strengthening with chin tucks, and single-leg balance**. Adam Menner's supine 90/90 breathing and single-leg bridge holds, from HoopStudy's 8-week in-season program, slot in on B days. Every move except chin tucks, jumping jacks and Menner's supine drills already has a demo video in the app. Outside the routine, the research backs three habits more strongly than any single stretch: **standing and moving for 1-3 minutes every 30-45 minutes of desk work**; **two minutes of band shoulder work on most workdays**, the one desk dose with trial evidence (about 40% less neck/shoulder pain in 10 weeks); and **heavier calf, knee, hamstring and groin work two or three times a week**, because tendons and strength respond to load, not daily light reps. The basketball coaches mostly contribute the *format*: short, daily, follow-along, habit-tracked, with heavy tendon loading kept to a few days a week. The app's current daily card is a reasonable start, but it is fixed, pure range of motion, and silent during the workday, so it should become an A/B flow with workday break prompts. As a general rule, stop any move that hurts sharply, and see a professional for pain that persists.

## Sitting is a reason to move often, not a posture to fix

The usual desk-worker story says sitting shortens the hip flexors, switches off the glutes and wrecks the back and neck. The evidence is more modest. In a cross-sectional study of 144 people, the most active, least-sitting group had **about 6° more passive hip extension** than the prolonged sitters. The study could not show that sitting caused the difference ([Boukabache et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33188982/)), but a 3-week hip-flexor stretching programme did raise passive hip extension ([IJSPT](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/137692)). That is a reasonable case for a daily hip-flexor stretch. A physiotherapy review found **very little evidence that sitting permanently deactivates the glutes** ("gluteal amnesia") ([Wild Physio](https://wildphysiofitness.au/blogs/physiotherapy/should-you-worry-about-gluteal-amnesia)). A causation-focused systematic review concluded that **occupational sitting alone is unlikely to cause low back pain** ([IASP summary of Roffey et al.](https://www.iasp-pain.org/publications/relief-news/article/back-pain-myths-closing-sale-everything-must-go/)). Slumped neck posture at 17 did not predict persistent neck pain at 22 in males ([Curtin University](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/new-research-finds-slumped-posture-not-such-a-pain-in-the-neck-after-all/)). What sitting clearly does is harm cardiometabolic health. The WHO's 2020 guideline makes a strong recommendation to replace sedentary time with activity of **any intensity** ([WHO guideline table, PMC7719906](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/table/T4)).

So the routine is built to move every joint through its full range daily, to strengthen the areas with trial evidence (neck/shoulders, trunk, ankle balance), and to break up sitting. It is not built to "correct posture." Hip range gets particular attention. Reduced hip internal rotation and total rotation are the ROM measures most consistently linked to hip and groin pain in athletes ([Tak et al. 2018](https://www.pubmed.gov/29449206/); [PMC5754850](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5754850)), although screening predicts poorly ([BJSM 51:1611](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/51/22/1611)). Range gains depend on **weekly accumulated stretching time**, at about 5-10 minutes per muscle group per week ([Thomas et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29506306/); [Sports Medicine 2025 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39614059/)). Roughly 45-90 seconds a day per area reaches that. Gains also fade within weeks once stretching stops ([detraining meta-analysis 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12638565/)). Mobility is therefore the part of the plan that has to be daily.

## Sackmann's flow, plus Menner's recovery work, plus the desk essentials

### What the two HoopStudy routines contain

The template comes from two HoopStudy primary sources the user supplied. ### The daily routine, A and B

The recommended routine keeps Sackmann's shape (stand, floor, lunge-and-squat) and his short-dose, follow-along feel. It condenses his 24 moves into **12 moves of about 45-75 seconds each, about 12 minutes in total**. That is close to his 12:35 and more than the 5-7 moves first planned, which is justified because each one is a short piece of a single flow, not a separate exercise to set up. It adds what neither HoopStudy routine has: **band neck/shoulder strengthening with chin tucks**, the best-evidenced desk item, and **progressive single-leg balance**, the best-evidenced injury-prevention item. Menner's supine work fills the B-day breathing and bridge slots. Five slots alternate, so each region gets a slightly different stimulus and the routine stays fresh.

### Why each block is there

**Ankles and calves first.** Calf-strain risk rises with age and previous strain ([Green & Pizzari 2017](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/51/16/1189.abstract)), and a coach stands and walks all day. Light daily calf and ankle work keeps the area moving, and heavier loading comes later in the week. **Dead bug and the 90/90 breathing** give trunk control and a breathing reset that overlap with McGill's spine-sparing set ([MUSC Health](https://muschealth.org/content-hub/News/2022/12/20/the-big-three)). Exercise in general has moderate-quality evidence for reducing future low back pain ([de Campos et al., BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/13/766.full.pdf)). **The 90/90** targets hip rotation, the ROM variable most tied to hip and groin trouble. **World's Greatest Stretch, the couch or half-kneeling stretch, and the deep squat** cover hip extension, T-spine rotation and deep flexion. These are the three positions sitting takes away, and they echo Patrick's couch stretch ([Knee Ability series](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-knee-ability-series-3-of-10)) and Starrett's couch test and floor-sitting practice ([mindbodydad](https://www.mindbodydad.com/body/built-to-move-vital-signs); [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/2023/10/17/23921547/why-you-should-sit-on-the-floor-daily-built-to-move/)). At 45 s per side a day, the hip-flexor stretch alone reaches the 5-10 min/week dose for range gains. **Band work and chin tucks** have the best desk evidence: in a review of 27 RCTs, neck/shoulder strengthening reduced neck pain in office workers, and more participation meant bigger effects ([Chen et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6093121/)). **Single-leg balance** comes last because it has the strongest injury-prevention evidence of anything in the routine. Balance training cut ankle-sprain risk by **35-46%**, most in people with a previous sprain ([Bellows & Wong 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6044595); [Hupperets et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19589822/)). Those trials ran 3-5 times a week, so daily practice is within range.

Two app details matter. The calf-raise rows only pick up their videos if the routine uses the catalog names exactly, "(Optional)" suffix included. And **CARs** (hip, shoulder, neck) are a good later addition, at 2-3 slow reps per direction ([Perfect Stride PT](https://perfectstridept.com/blogs/controlled-articular-rotations-cars/)), but the app has no CARs entries yet.

## Every 30-45 minutes, stand up for one to three minutes

The break schedule comes from metabolic research. Breaking up sitting every 30 minutes with 3-minute bouts of light walking or simple bodyweight moves improved post-meal markers ([Dempsey et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27208318/)). For pain, a 12-week RCT of an "Active Break" programme in young adults with low back pain during prolonged sitting improved pain and disability ([PEDro 80906](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/80906)). Sit-stand desks cut sitting by about **57-100 minutes a day**, but their health benefits are unproven ([Cochrane, Shrestha et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29926475/)), so alternating every 30-45 minutes beats standing all day.

| Break | Content (app names) | Video |
|---|---|---|
| **1 min** "reset" | Stand and walk to refill water. 10 slow calf raises. 5 standing glute squeezes | catalog |
| **2 min** "band desk dose" (once or twice a day) | Lateral Raise (band) 15, or Band Pull-Aparts 15, plus Band External Rotations 10/side and chin tucks* 5 × 5 s. Keep the band at the desk and progress it when 15 reps are easy | catalog |
| **2 min** "spine and wrists" | Wall Slides 8-10, Wall Side T Spine Opener 3/side, and Sackmann's 3 Way Palm Stretch* × 5 | catalog / pjf |
| **2 min** "hips" | Half Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch 30 s/side, then 30 s of Asian Squat | pjf / catalog |
| **3 min** "walk" | Brisk walk, a phone call on foot, or stairs. Add 30-60 s of Backward Walk where there is room | catalog |

The 2-minute band option rests on one RCT: **2 minutes a day, 5 days a week**, of progressive elastic-tubing work cut neck/shoulder pain by about 40% in 10 weeks, as effectively as 12 minutes a day ([Andersen et al., University of Copenhagen](https://www.ucviden.dk/en/publications/effect-of-brief-daily-resistance-training-on-occupational-necksho/)). A secondary analysis also found fewer headaches ([OHS Online](https://ohsonline.com/articles/2011/06/12/brief-daily-resistance-training-eases-headaches.aspx)). The participants were mostly women who already had pain, and the exact exercise ("lateral raise with tubing") is reported, not verified from the full text. For wrists, an Institute for Work & Health review found moderate evidence for stretching programmes and strong evidence for workplace resistance training in preventing and managing upper-limb disorders ([IWH](https://iwh.on.ca/node/1399)). The 30-45 minute interval is an inference from metabolic studies. No trial has set the best break frequency for joint stiffness in adults over 35.

## Tendons and strength need load two or three times a week

Some of what keeps a 40-year-old's legs healthy does not respond to daily light work. About **75% of calf injuries in English FA data involved the soleus** ([FMPA](https://www.fmpa.co.uk/app/uploads/2026/03/Under-the-surface.pdf)). The Achilles adapts only to high-strain loading at about 90% of maximal effort ([Arampatzis 2007](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/210/15/2743/17005/Adaptational-responses-of-the-human-Achilles)). It still adapts in older adults, but over about 14 weeks ([Epro/Karamanidis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5835978)). PJF reportedly calls tib raises "overrated" next to soleus and calf work ([Recapio summary of PJF ep. 058](https://recapio.com/digest/058-knee-health-myths-truth-about-tib-raises-stretching-and-more-pjf-podcast-by-pjfperformance)). Ben Patrick keeps them as strength work at about 2 × 15 ([Knee Ability series](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-knee-ability-series-3-of-10)). This block is a 10-12 minute add-on after the daily routine on two or three days a week, for example Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday.

His full recovery sequence works well as a stand-alone evening or Sunday session. Plyometric tendon work such as PJF's "springs" drill (3-4 × 20 s per leg, 3-5 days a week; [PJF](https://www.pjfperformance.com/build-your-springs-achilles-foot-strength-elasticity-and-stability-drill/)) is optional for someone who is not training to play.

## Coaching days need a five-minute primer; desk days need the breaks

On **coaching days** his feet, calves and knees carry the load. He should keep the morning routine, using the 3-minute minimum version below if time is short. Before practice he adds a 4-5 minute primer: Sackmann's **Pogos** × 25 and jumping jacks × 25; **Backward Walk** 1-2 min (catalog; Patrick's entry-level knee tool, [SetForSet](https://www.setforset.com/blogs/news/joe-rogan-kneesovertoesguy-ben-patrick-how-to-fix-knee-pain)); **World's Greatest Stretch** 3/side; **Leg Swings** 8/side front-to-back and side-to-side; and **Band Pull-Aparts** 10. If his knees are cranky he adds **60 Degree Knee ISOs** 2 × 30 s before standing for hours. Menner's tissue work (Optional Foam Roll Circuit, 30 s per area) is a reasonable add-on, because foam rolling is better justified as a warm-up tool than for recovery ([Wiewelhove 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31024339/)). Alan Stein's sequence of pre-hab, then a dynamic warm-up ([PlaySports TV](https://www.playsportstv.com/basketball/warm-ups-drills)), fits this. So does Sackmann's habit of following the physical prep with a short ball-skill block ([DJ Sackmann on X](https://x.com/DJSackmann/status/1878837426837147878)), which suits a coach who demonstrates. After practice, 2 minutes of Menner's supine 90/90 breathing makes a good wind-down.

On **desk-heavy days** the whole plan applies: the full A or B routine in the morning, a break every 30-45 minutes, the 2-minute band dose once or twice, and one real walk. PJF's "10 daily habits" episode makes the same case, with movement snacks every hour and 10k steps ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/nz/podcast/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance/id1462156813); [PJF on X](https://x.com/pjfperformance?lang=en)).

## Sixty-six days to automatic: build it as a habit

HoopStudy's main contribution is its format, and the Day 10 recording shows what that format is: a **day-numbered, ~12-minute follow-along video** with many moves at short doses, flowing from standing to floor and back up. There are no decisions to make, because he presses play and follows. HoopStudy's mobility products are sold on "the habit and accountability of prioritizing mobility" ([Playbook: Hoop Mobility](https://my.playbookapp.io/dj-sackmann/programs/hoop-mobility/627)), and Sackmann calls his own fixed morning routine "very important" ([Authority Magazine](https://medium.com/authority-magazine/how-dj-sackmann-thrives-as-an-athlete-turned-entrepreneur-521afa053d01)). Habit research supports the design. In Lally et al., 96 adults repeated a daily behaviour tied to a cue. Automaticity took a **median of 66 days** (range 18-254). The biggest gains came early, and **missing a single day did not materially hurt**, but very inconsistent people never formed the habit ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)).

The practical rules follow from that. He should **pin the routine to one fixed cue**, such as straight after the morning coffee, on the same mat. He should keep a **3-minute minimum version** for bad days: Dead Bug 5 breaths, 90/90 Hip Opener 1 min, Band Pull-Aparts 15. He should treat a miss as "never miss twice", not a broken streak, and expect the routine to feel automatic at around **two months**, not two weeks. A monthly re-test turns it into feedback: Starrett's couch test, arms-overhead "Airport Scanner" check, sit-and-rise test and single-leg "Old Man" balance test ([mindbodydad](https://www.mindbodydad.com/body/built-to-move-vital-signs)), plus Squat University's 5-inch knee-to-wall ankle screen ([Squat University](https://squatuniversity.com/2015/11/05/the-squat-fix-ankle-mobility-pt-1/)).

## The current daily mobility card is a static checklist

The app's "Daily mobility" card shows a fixed BWS list from `library.json → warmups.bws_daily_mobility`: Cat Cow 60 s, World's Greatest Stretch 30 s/side, Asian Squat 30 s hold plus 30 s side-to-side, Half Kneeling Thoracic Rotations 30 s, and Wall Slides 60 s ([daily_mobility.js](../src/views/daily_mobility.js), lines 20-24; [library.json](../data/library.json), line 61035). The moves are good, and two of them carry over into the new routine. The card has five problems. **First, it is pure range of motion.** Nothing trains ankles and calves, balance, the trunk, or neck/shoulder strength, which are the items with the best evidence for a coach at a desk. It also has no hip rotation (90/90) or dedicated hip-flexor stretch. **Second, it never changes**: no rotation and no day-type logic. **Third, it is a checklist rather than a flow**, the opposite of the follow-along format that makes HoopStudy's routine easy to repeat. **Fourth, it does nothing during the workday**, where the long sitting bouts happen. **Fifth, its "Add exercise" picker** takes the first 40 names matching `/mobility|durability|stretch|warm/` ([daily_mobility.js](../src/views/daily_mobility.js), lines 131-173), not tags. Logs are also stored per device only (lines 9-18).

## What this rests on, and what is still missing

Most evidence here comes from **search-engine snippets and summaries**, not full texts, because the session's network blocked nearly every primary coach page (PJF, Knees Over Toes' Substack, Prehab Guys, Squat University's newer pages, Boostcamp, YouTube). Doses attributed to coaches should be checked against the source. The two primary coach sources are the user's own. No source gives a protocol tailored to men around 40. Most injury-prevention trials studied youth or elite athletes, and most joint-specific doses (CARs, T-spine drills, ankle work) rest on practitioner convention, not trials.

## Conclusion

The routine that suits this coach is not a new invention. It is Sackmann's own ten-minute flow, condensed and finished with the two things the desk evidence most supports and HoopStudy leaves out: band shoulder work and balance. Menner's supine recovery drills give the floor block a breathing reset. Neither coach's moves are the weak link. The real gaps are dose and timing: heavy calf, hamstring and groin work belongs on two or three days, and sitting needs breaking up all day, not undoing once in the morning.

For the app, the change goes beyond swapping exercises. It turns the mobility card from a static checklist into a follow-along A/B flow that also nudges him during the workday, where most of his sitting happens. The next steps are to record one or two more Sackmann days to widen the move pool, check Menner's workbook images, and add the handful of missing exercises with videos.
