INTRODUCTION

There is a common misconception that eating healthy
is expensive and takes a lot of time. While fruits,
vegetables, and lean proteins may have a higher price
tag compared to many boxed and manufactured
foods, there are plenty of creative ways to eat
nutritious foods that don’t break the bank or eat up
your time.


High-quality nutrition is important for a multitude
of reasons. The foods we eat provide both
macronutrients, such as protein, carbohydrates, and
fats, as well as micronutrients such as iron, vitamin
D, B vitamins, etc. When we don’t consistently supply
our bodies with the foods and nutrients it needs,
we can be left hungry, with low energy, and needing
“more” to fill the gaps.


Also, it is important to redefine your definition of
healthy and remember that ALL foods can fit into a
healthy and balanced nutrition plan! Utilizing the
following tips to save money while shopping, we
will show you how to build balanced meals using
inexpensive and nutritious fridge and pantry staples.

TIPS FOR SUPERMARKET SAVINGS

            Fresh & Frozen Finds
            Frozen fruits and vegetables are frozen at peak freshness and have the same
            nutritional value and quality as fresh options. Opt for frozen if you feel your fresh
            produce goes bad quickly. Canned is a convenient option due to the shelf life, but try
            to aim for finding a low sodium option.
            Be Sale Savvy
            Sign up for your grocery store’s points/reward system. They frequently have special
            savings, discounts, or extra discounts if you’re a member. These savings can add up
            over time.
            Store Brand Savings
            Opt for store brand or generic items over name brand to save some money. These
            products are very similar in terms of nutrition, quality, taste and usually are much
            cheaper.
            Whole Foods & Wholesale
            Look to buy foods in whole or bulk rather than those that are more prepared. For
            example, choose a whole block of cheese and shred it yourself rather than buying
            the pre shredded option. You can also save money on meat this way as well. Buy bulk
            ground meat and portion into 1 or 2 lb servings, freezing what you won’t use right
            away.
            If you have a membership to a wholesale club, get your shelf stable products here.
            By buying those products in bulk you’ll save money, and you’ll have a good stock of
            products.
            Shop Seasonally
            Depending on the season, some produce will be cheaper than others due to higher
            amounts being grown and its availability. Look online to see what produce is in season
            near you and plan to use those in your recipes.
            Plan for Success
            Plan your meals for the week ahead of time and use recipes that have common
            ingredients. This is most helpful for getting the most bang for your buck on produce.
            It ensures nothing goes bad or goes to waste because you’ll use it for multiple recipes.
