## 1. Split choice by days per week

### Takeaway
Ethier's default is full body 3x/week, upper/lower at 4 days and PPL at 5-6 days, on the principle that each muscle should be trained about 2x/week. Nippard sells the same content in several frequency versions. For example, Min-Max Phase 2 uses Upper/Lower/Push/Pull at 4 days and adds an Arms day at 5 days.

### Cited Findings
- Ethier (January 2026 article): a full body routine "3x/week gives you the best results in the least amount of time", because it exposes each muscle to a growth stimulus more often than a once-a-week split — [BWS full body routine, official](https://builtwithscience.com/workouts/full-body-workout-routine/)
- Ethier: when volume is matched, training each muscle twice a week gives "significantly greater muscle growth" than once a week. The BWS upper/lower plan is written for 4x/week — [BWS best workout split, official](https://builtwithscience.com/workouts/best-workout-split/)
- Secondary summary of Ethier: upper/lower suits 4 days/week, and PPL suits 5-6 days/week because it keeps sessions from getting too long and fatiguing — [BoxRox, secondary](https://www.boxrox.com/best-science-based-workout-split-to-maximise-muscle-growth/)
- A coaching breakdown says Ethier holds that the split matters less than people think, so it should be chosen from the days available — [Coachway, secondary](https://coachway.io/articles/jeremy-ethier-how-to-design-a-training-program/)
- Nippard Min-Max Phase 2: 4-day split is Upper / Lower / Push / Pull, 5-day split is Upper / Lower / Push / Pull / Arms, and sessions run about 45 min — [jeffnippard.com Min-Max Phase 2, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/min-max-program-phase-2)
- Nippard Fundamentals Hypertrophy Program comes in full-body, upper/lower and body-part-split versions. The body-part version can be run 5 days in a row with weekends off — [Fundamentals product page, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/fundamentals-hypertrophy-program); [unofficial PDF copy](https://kevindelaplante.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/JeffNippardsFundamentalsHypertrophyProgram.pdf)
- Nippard also sells a PPL hypertrophy program for intermediate/advanced lifters — [jeffnippard.com PPL, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/push-pull-legs-hypertrophy-program). Pure Bodybuilding is distributed in PPL and Upper/Lower versions — [Scribd copy PB PPL](https://www.scribd.com/document/714876627/Purebodybuilding-Phase-2-PPL); [Scribd copy PB U/L](https://www.scribd.com/document/730169778/Pure-Bodybuilding-Upper-Lower)

### Inferences
- A rule set consistent with both coaches: 2-3 days = full body, 4 days = upper/lower (or Nippard's U/L/Push/Pull hybrid), 5 days = U/L + PPL hybrid or U/L/Push/Pull/Arms, 6 days = PPL x2. Every option is built to train each muscle at least 2x/week.
- The Arms day in Nippard's 5x version suggests the extra day goes to small or "weak point" muscles rather than another full repeat of the split.

### Gaps
- I could not read Nippard's own split mapping for the 2x/3x/4x/5x Fundamentals versions, or the Pure Bodybuilding Full Body/U-L/PPL day counts, in full.
- I found no Ethier statement for 2 days/week or 6 days/week PPL specifics. A November 2026 Ethier email mentions a "6 days/week training" experiment, but its content was not visible.

## 2. Weekly sets per muscle and set counting

### Takeaway
Both coaches have moved toward lower volume with very hard sets. Nippard's minimalist programs use 1-2 hard sets per exercise taken to 0-1 RIR, and he reports benefits from as little as 1-4 sets/week. He has historically worked in the conventional ~10-20 sets/week range. He cites the Pelland et al. meta-analysis, which counts indirect sets as 0.5 ("fractional sets").

### Cited Findings
- Nippard cites Schoenfeld 2017: 1-4 sets per muscle per week produced 64% of the hypertrophy of 10+ sets — [Nippard on X](https://x.com/JeffNippard/status/1585324605480501248); the Essentials page says "even just 1-4 sets per muscle per week was enough to stimulate measurable hypertrophy" — [Essentials Program, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/the-essentials-program)
- Essentials: "most isolation exercises will be limited to just 1-2 hard sets per exercise" — [Essentials Program, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/the-essentials-program)
- Min-Max: "1–2 hard working sets on most exercises, often taken to 0–1 reps in reserve" — [Min-Max Phase 2, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/min-max-program-phase-2)
- In a 100-day experiment Nippard averaged 6.5 sets/muscle/week, against the ~10-20 sets/week he followed before — [Boxlife Magazine, secondary](https://boxlifemagazine.com/science-backed-single-set-training-protocol/); [Nextcult, secondary](https://nextcultbrand.com/blogs/gym-workout-fitness/can-three-sets-really-be-enough-jeff-nippard-and-the-case-for-lower-training-volume)
- Pelland et al. (Sports Medicine, doi 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w) counts 1 set for the target muscle and 0.5 for each assisting muscle. In that paper, fractional counting predicted growth best. Hypertrophy keeps rising with volume but with diminishing returns from around 10 weekly sets, while strength plateaus near ~4 fractional sets/week — [Pelland PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf); [preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/537/1148/1071); summary [FitChef, secondary](https://fitchef.com/studies/training-volume-sets-per-week-study/). A secondary source quotes Nippard calling it "the biggest, most detailed meta-analysis on training volume that's ever been published" — [Boxlife, secondary](https://boxlifemagazine.com/science-backed-single-set-training-protocol/)
- BWS has an article on weekly sets per muscle, titled to the effect that "less = more". It discusses a 2016 study comparing <12 sets with higher volumes and a 2017 meta-analysis showing a set-volume dose response — [BWS sets per week, official](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/how-many-sets-per-muscle-group-per-week/); [BWS how many sets](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/how-many-sets-should-i-do/)
- During a 7-day very-low-calorie experiment, Ethier cut to 1 hard set per exercise, reasoning that maintaining muscle takes about 1/3 of the volume needed to build it — [Boxlife, secondary](https://boxlifemagazine.com/lose-fat-3x-faster/)

### Inferences
- A generator could count volume fractionally (1.0 direct, 0.5 indirect), following Pelland, the paper Nippard cites. Explicit targets in that counting would be roughly: maintenance ~1/3 of build volume; minimalist ~4-6 sets; standard ~10-12 sets, where returns start to diminish; high ~15-20 sets. This tiering is my inference, not a published BWS/Nippard table.
- Beginner, intermediate and advanced lifters are separated mainly by program choice: Fundamentals/Essentials for beginners, PPL/Pure Bodybuilding for intermediate/advanced. The products don't publish a sets-per-level table.

### Gaps
- I could not read BWS's exact per-level set numbers (the article page fetch was blocked). There is no confirmed official BWS beginner/intermediate/advanced table.
- I couldn't confirm whether BWS or Nippard programs count sets fractionally themselves, as opposed to citing research that does.

## 3. Exercise selection, order, tiers, exercises per session

### Takeaway
Nippard ranks exercises in public S-to-F tier lists that strongly favour exercises loading the muscle at long lengths (stretch). He uses lengthened partials mainly on the last set. Minimalist programs use few exercises with 1-2 hard sets each.

### Cited Findings
- Biceps S tier: 45° preacher curl and face-away Bayesian cable curl. Incline curl is A tier ("solid lengthened bias") — [Fitness Volt, secondary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-ranks-best-and-worst-bicep-exercises/)
- Chest S tier: machine chest press and seated cable fly. Barbell bench is A tier, with less stretch than dumbbells — [BarBend, secondary](https://barbend.com/news/jeff-nippard-ranks-chest-exercises-for-hypertrophy/)
- Back S tier: pulldown variations and chest-supported rows — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/jeff-nippard-ranks-best-and-worst-back-exercises/). Quads S tier: hack squat and Bulgarian split squat — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/best-quad-exercises-ranking-best-to-worst-jeff-nippard/). Side delts: single-arm cable lateral raise at the top — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/best-worst-shoulder-exercises-tier-list/). Triceps S tier: overhead extensions, barbell skull crushers — [BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/jeff-nippard-ranks-best-and-worst-triceps-exercises/)
- Lengthened partials: used mainly as a last-set intensity technique, taking the set to failure at full ROM and then adding about 3-6 partial reps. He also describes "integrated partials" and all-partial sets, and does not use them on every set. He helped run a 2024 study of full ROM vs lengthened partials — [Fitness Volt, secondary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-long-length-partials-muscle-growth/); [Nippard on X](https://x.com/JeffNippard/status/1842249599568036010)
- A Pure Bodybuilding U/L copy lists long-length partials on all reps of the last set — [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/730169778/Pure-Bodybuilding-Upper-Lower)
- Fundamentals defines "primary exercises" as squat, bench press, deadlift and overhead press, which get specific warm-ups — [Fundamentals PDF copy](https://kevindelaplante.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/JeffNippardsFundamentalsHypertrophyProgram.pdf)

### Inferences
- Order rule (inferred from programs, not stated in the sources found): primary compound first, then secondary compounds, then isolations. Lengthened-bias isolations and machine/cable picks fill the accessory slots.
- An exercise library could carry a "tier" score and a "lengthened-bias" flag per exercise, with the generator preferring S/A tier picks.

### Gaps
- I found no explicit statement on exercise order or on exercises per muscle per session from either coach.

## 4. Rep ranges, proximity to failure, RIR across a block

### Takeaway
Nippard programs train close to failure. Early sets sit at slightly lower RPE, and the last set goes to failure, except in intro/deload weeks. Most working sets after week 1 are RPE 9-10. Min-Max Phase 2 moved failure to the first working set. Isolation exercises go to true failure, while big compounds stop shy of it.

### Cited Findings
- Bodybuilding Transformation System: early sets are "a little bit easier (slightly lower RPE)", and the last set goes to failure on all exercises except intro/deload weeks. Week 1 is an intro/deload, after which most sets are RPE 9-10 — [Boostcamp user upload, secondary](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/L46EDA-nippards-the-bodybuilding-transformation-system)
- Min-Max (older version): "nearly every set... taken to failure, or 1 rep shy of failure". On non-compound exercises, 0 RIR means you should actually attempt another rep. Phase 2: most exercises go to failure on the first working set instead of the last — [Boostcamp Min-Max, secondary](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/XIZgUP-jeff-nippards-min-max-4xweek); [Min-Max Phase 2, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/min-max-program-phase-2)
- Min-Max blocks: Block 1 (6 wk) covers consistency, execution and intensity. Block 2 (6 wk) adds last-set techniques: drop sets, myo-reps, static holds — [Boostcamp, secondary](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/p1xXzM-jeff-nippards-min-max)
- Fundamentals and PPL track LSRPE ("how hard was your LAST set?") — [PPL program page, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/push-pull-legs-hypertrophy-program); the PPL uses both RPE and %1RM prescriptions — [same](https://jeffnippard.com/products/push-pull-legs-hypertrophy-program)
- Ethier's example double progression uses 3 sets of 8-12 reps. He says that if load is limited, you can keep the weight and push reps as high as ~30 as long as sets stay hard — [BWS progressive overload, official](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/progressive-overload-techniques/)

### Inferences
- A rule set: compounds stay at RPE 8-9 (1-2 RIR), isolations go to RPE 9-10 / 0 RIR, and only the last set of each exercise goes to failure. Week 1 of a block runs at reduced RPE. Advanced or late-block weeks add one technique on the last set (lengthened partials, drop set, myo-reps).
- Rep ranges in Nippard's programs are not fully documented in the sources found. A typical pattern of heavier 4-8 on compounds and 8-15 on isolations is an inference.

### Gaps
- I found no exact Pure Bodybuilding early-set RPE targets (such as 7-8). Official rep ranges per exercise type were not readable.

## 5. Rest periods

### Takeaway
BWS's article recommends at least ~3 min for heavy compounds and ~2 min for isolations. The BWS app's timer defaults are shorter.

### Cited Findings
- BWS: about 2 min was enough for single-joint exercises, while heavy compounds (bench, OHP, squat) did best with at least 3 min — [BWS rest between sets, official](https://builtwithscience.com/workouts/rest-between-sets/)
- BWS app rest timer defaults (as summarised by search): isolation about 30-60 s, compound about 90 s-2 min — [BWS support: rest timer, official](https://builtwithscience-support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39351660348571-How-does-the-rest-timer-work). This conflicts with the article.

### Inferences
- Default rule: compounds 3 min, isolations 2 min. An optional "time-efficient" mode could use the app defaults.

### Gaps
- I found no Nippard rest-period specifics. The snippet values for the app defaults could not be checked against the full page.

## 6. Progression model and block length

### Takeaway
BWS uses double progression: add reps to the top of the range, then add load and drop back to the bottom. Nippard's programs use RPE/LSRPE-based load progression, 12-week programs split into blocks of about 6 weeks, and fixed exercises for about 4 weeks before rotating (Essentials).

### Cited Findings
- Ethier's double progression: 3x8-12 at 100 lb, add reps weekly until the top of the range, then add 10 lb and go back to 8 reps — [BWS progressive overload, official](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/progressive-overload-techniques/)
- Fundamentals: keep the load until its RPE drops by about 1 point, then increase — [Scribd copy, secondary](https://www.scribd.com/document/861811586/Jeff-Nippard-Hypertrophy-Fundamentals-Program)
- Essentials: "progress with the same exercises for four weeks before they are swapped out" — [Essentials Program, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/the-essentials-program)
- Min-Max: 12 weeks as two 6-week blocks — [Boostcamp, secondary](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/p1xXzM-jeff-nippards-min-max). PPL: Block 2 starts with a deload — [PPL page, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/push-pull-legs-hypertrophy-program)
- Nippard progresses abs by about +1 rep per week or added weight — [Fitness Volt, secondary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-6-pack-abs-tips/)

### Inferences
- Rules: double progression within a rep range (+reps, then +load of about 2.5-5% / the smallest plate). Rotate accessory exercises every 4-6 weeks. Run blocks of about 6 weeks, with week 1 as an intro. Nothing found says to add sets mid-block. Both coaches lean toward intensity over volume, so "add a set" should be a late-block option at most (inference).

### Gaps
- I found no explicit rule on when to add sets. Pure Bodybuilding phase lengths were not confirmed.

## 7. Deloads

### Takeaway
BWS (2019) suggests a deload week every 4-8 weeks, or autoregulated deloads when symptoms appear. Nippard builds deload/intro weeks into his blocks: week 1, and the start of Block 2 in PPL.

### Cited Findings
- Ethier: schedule "a full deload week... every 4-8 weeks or so" depending on age and training history, or deload when the body "feels" overtrained. He notes little formal research, citing a 2013 Tokyo study (Ogasawara) in which periodic detraining matched continuous training over 24 weeks — [BWS deload week, official (2019)](https://builtwithscience.com/fitness-tips/deload-week/)
- Nippard: intro/deload week 1, during which the last set is not taken to failure — [Boostcamp, secondary](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/L46EDA-nippards-the-bodybuilding-transformation-system). The PPL Block 2 opens with a deload "to emphasize recovery leading into the most... demanding phase" — [PPL page, official](https://jeffnippard.com/products/push-pull-legs-hypertrophy-program)

### Inferences
- Implementation: every 5-7 weeks (or week 1 of each new block), keep the exercises and remove failure/techniques (RPE about 6-7). The cut is mainly to intensity, plus some volume. I found no source giving the exact volume cut, and third-party AI-like guides disagreed.

### Gaps
- I found no official volume-reduction percentage.

## 8. Warm-ups, core, cardio, fat-loss modifications

### Takeaway
Nippard: a 5-10 min general warm-up plus dynamic mobility, then exercise-specific ramp sets (reps fall as load rises) only for primary lifts. That is 3-4+ ramp sets for heavy (1-7 rep) work and 2-3 for lighter work. Abs: about 2 exercises x 3 sets, 2x/week, with the last set to failure. Ethier treats cardio (mostly walking, 5k-10k steps) as a supplement and recommends 0.5-1% bodyweight loss/week.

### Cited Findings
- Fundamentals: an exercise-specific warm-up only for squat, bench, deadlift and OHP. Warm-up sets "are not meant to be challenging", and reps go down as weight goes up. Use 3-4+ warm-up sets for heavier work (~1-7 reps) and 2-3 for lighter, higher-rep work — [Fundamentals PDF copy](https://kevindelaplante.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/JeffNippardsFundamentalsHypertrophyProgram.pdf); [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/861811586/Jeff-Nippard-Hypertrophy-Fundamentals-Program)
- Nippard's push-day example: 45x10-15, 135x5, 185x3, 225x2 — [Fitness Volt, secondary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-push-workout/). His warm-up video says to raise body temperature, take active joints through full ROM, ramp to working weight, and prefer dynamic over static stretching — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/the-most-effective-science-based-warm-up-mobility-routine-full-body)
- An Ethier-inspired fan program uses ramps only on the first compound (45x10, 95x8, 135x5, 185x3 for a 225 squat) and none for accessories. This is not official — [Boostcamp, fan-made](https://www.boostcamp.app/users/WPECKg-jeremy-ethier-inspired-hypertrophy)
- Nippard abs: weighted crunch + leg raise, about 3x10-12 (leg raise 10-20), 2x/week, last set of crunches to failure, days flexible — [Fitness Volt, secondary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-6-pack-abs-tips/); [Generation Iron](https://generationiron.com/jeff-nippard-get-abs-by-summer/)
- Ethier on fat loss: full-body strength training is the main way to keep muscle. Cardio is supplementary, mostly walking from about 5,000 to 10,000 steps/day. At very low body fat, cutting calories further or adding training can backfire. Target loss is about 0.5-1% bodyweight/week — [BoxRox, secondary](https://www.boxrox.com/its-tough-but-it-gets-you-abs-in-60-days-says-jeremy-ethier/); [BoxRox cardio](https://www.boxrox.com/how-much-cardio-should-you-do-to-lose-fat-according-to-science/)
- Ethier's 30-day running experiment: run after lifting, raise carbs, protect leg-day performance. MRI showed no upper-body muscle loss — [BoxRox, secondary](https://www.boxrox.com/how-running-every-day-changed-jeremy-ethiers-body-without-killing-his-gains/)

### Inferences
- Ramp template for primary lifts (inferred from the Fundamentals rules and Nippard's example): about 40-50% x 8-10, 60-70% x 4-5, 80-85% x 2-3, and about 90% x 1-2 when the working set is ≤7 reps.
- Cut mode: keep the exercises and intensity, optionally reduce volume toward maintenance (≥1/3 of build volume), add daily steps, and do cardio after lifting or on separate days.

### Gaps
- I found no official BWS warm-up percentages and no explicit Nippard/BWS cardio minutes or HR zones. The user's example ramp (50%x8 / 70%x3-4 / 90%x1-2) was not confirmed verbatim in any source.

## 9. BWS app adaptive features

### Takeaway
The BWS+ app says it adjusts weights and reps each session from logged performance and shows previous performance. The algorithm itself is not public.

### Cited Findings
- The app says it tells you exactly how to adjust weights and reps each session — [Google Play, official listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bws.appv2). It shows previous weights/reps — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/built-with-science/id6446000532). Version notes mention workouts that "adapt and change as you lift"; a user review says it uses logged data to refine targets — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/built-with-science/id6446000532?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)
- A third-party review says logged data sets next week's targets, and the app has a chat assistant with access to training data and a "Recomp Detector" — [Intercom-hosted review, secondary](https://intercom.help/insideverdict/en/articles/16381910-built-with-science-review-2026-i-tested-it-is-it-legit-worth-it)
- The app has a rest timer with defaults by exercise type — [BWS support](https://builtwithscience-support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/39351660348571-How-does-the-rest-timer-work)

### Inferences
- The app most likely runs double progression from the last session's logged reps and load. That is consistent with Ethier's published method but not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No documentation of the progression algorithm was found.
