# Protect the hip, train everything around it

The best daily routine for an almost-40 coach who sits at a computer is a **hip-safe version of DJ Sackmann's own HoopStudy "Ten Minute Routine"**: a roughly 10-11 minute follow-along flow of 12 short-dose moves, with five of them alternating between A and B days. It goes from a standing warm-up (marches, calf raises, ankle circles, arm circles) to a floor block (dead bugs, glute bridges, adductor ball squeezes, rock-back breathing or cat cow, yoga push-ups or bird dog) and back up to a standing finish (a wall T-spine drill, band pull-aparts or external rotations with chin tucks, single-leg balance). The user's screen recording of Sackmann's Day 10 shows 24 moves in 12:35. Sixteen are hip-friendly and stay. Eight sit on this hip's aggravators and get swapped: the 90/90 hold and transitions, the three Spider-Man lunge drills, Lunge × Reach, front and side lunges, and the deep squat. Every recommended move already has a demo video in the app, except chin tucks, which need adding. Outside the routine, the research supports three things: **frequent position changes during desk work** (stand and move for 1-3 minutes every 30-45 minutes); **two minutes of band shoulder work on most workdays**, the one desk dose with trial evidence (about 40% less neck/shoulder pain in 10 weeks); and **heavier calf, knee and hamstring work two or three times a week**, because tendons and strength respond to load, not daily light reps. His hip shapes the content more than any coaching method. The flare began in October 2026 after a pull in a 90/90 stretch, and sitting, lunges and forward-driving hip-flexor stretches make it worse. So the app's current daily card, which includes World's Greatest Stretch and Asian Squat, should be replaced. This is not medical advice. If the hip has not clearly settled by about six weeks after onset (early December 2026), or if any red flag below appears, he should see his PT.

## Sitting is the trigger to manage, not the injury to stretch away

The usual desk-worker story says sitting shortens the hip flexors, switches off the glutes and wrecks the back, so you stretch the front of the hip. The evidence is weaker than that story. In a cross-sectional study of 144 people, the most active, least-sitting group had **about 6° more passive hip extension** than the prolonged sitters. The study could not show that sitting caused the difference, and it found no link to pain ([Boukabache et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33188982/)). A physiotherapy review of "gluteal amnesia" found **very little evidence that sitting permanently deactivates the glutes**. It notes the label often gets attached to gluteal tendon problems on the outside of the hip, which are a different condition ([Wild Physio](https://wildphysiofitness.au/blogs/physiotherapy/should-you-worry-about-gluteal-amnesia)). A causation-focused systematic review concluded that **occupational sitting alone is unlikely to cause low back pain** ([IASP summary of Roffey et al.](https://www.iasp-pain.org/publications/relief-news/article/back-pain-myths-closing-sale-everything-must-go/)). Slumped neck posture at 17 did not predict persistent neck pain at 22 in males ([Curtin University](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/new-research-finds-slumped-posture-not-such-a-pain-in-the-neck-after-all/)). What sitting clearly does is harm cardiometabolic health. The WHO's 2020 guideline makes a strong recommendation to replace sedentary time with activity of **any intensity** ([WHO guideline table, PMC7719906](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/table/T4)). So the routine should change position often, build strength, and move through comfortable range. It should not try to "correct posture" or stretch the front of the hip hard.

That matters for him because his hip-flexor stretches *provoke* pain. That looks like irritable tissue or an irritable joint, not simple tightness. The app records the flare as "burning/pinching on the side and back of the hip, glute and outer-thigh tightness". It started after a pull in a 90/90 stretch, gets worse with lunges, sitting and the forward-driving hip-flexor stretch, and eases when he lies down. The cause is "not established yet" ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), lines 1759-1760). The pattern overlaps with several conditions, and search-level research cannot tell them apart. In FAI syndrome, UK hip-physio guidance advises modifying prolonged and cross-legged sitting and deep hip flexion ([British Hip Society](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)), and NHS guidance says to **avoid repeated stretching into the painful range** ([Wiltshire NHS](https://wiltshirehealthandcare.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2024/10/Femoroacetabular-Impingement-FAI-Syndrome-100.0.pdf)). In iliopsoas-related groin pain, guidance favours isometrics and checking that nothing flares symptoms over 24 hours ([NHS GGC](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/ggc-msk-index/hip/anterior-groin-iliopsoas)). Gluteal tendinopathy causes lateral pain, and the app's own note warns that figure-4 positions may compress those tendons ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), line 120). Across all of these, the safe common ground is the same: no end-range stretching into pain, strength through comfortable range, a seat set with hips at or above knees, and pain during exercise of **no more than about 3/10 that settles within 24 hours**.

**What to pause while the hip is irritable:** 90/90 sits and transitions, mermaid, pigeon, couch stretch and PJF's Hip Flexor/Quad Bridge, half-kneeling hip-flexor stretches, World's Greatest Stretch, lunges and split squats (including split-stance isometrics), shin-box rotations, deep squat holds (Asian Squat), and cross-legged sitting. The app's flare mode already flags most of these by name ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), lines 1819-1828). It does not catch World's Greatest Stretch or the shin-box moves, which put the hip in the same positions. Side-lying load (side planks, clamshells, Copenhagens) also counts toward the side-hip weekly total. The app says "trimming the total side-hip volume is the first lever" ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), line 487). So the daily routine adds none.

**When to see the PT rather than self-manage:** any of the app's stop signs, which are a sharp pinch, burning that keeps building, tingling or numbness, weakness or a limp ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), lines 1777-1779). Also night pain that wakes him, groin pain with clicking or locking, pain radiating down the leg, or no clear improvement **4-6 weeks** after he changed what he does ([British Hip Society](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf); [NHS GGC](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/ggc-msk-index/hip/anterior-groin-iliopsoas)). The flare dates from October 2026, so that window closes around now. He should show the PT the A/B list below and ask specifically about the Thomas stretch, rock-back breathing and bird dog in quadruped, and when Nordics, small hip CARs and Sackmann's paused moves can come back.

FRC's rehab use is a "small pain-free range, ~10% tension" ([Perfect Stride PT](https://perfectstridept.com/blogs/controlled-articular-rotations-cars/)), but a full hip circle passes through the deep flexion and rotation that started the flare, and the app has no CARs entry yet.

## Every 30-45 minutes, stand up for one to three minutes

The break schedule comes from metabolic research, not joint research. Breaking up sitting every 30 minutes with 3-minute bouts of light walking or simple bodyweight moves improved post-meal markers ([Dempsey et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27208318/)). For pain, a 12-week RCT of an "Active Break" programme in young adults with low back pain during prolonged sitting improved pain and disability ([PEDro 80906](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/80906)). Sit-stand desks cut sitting by about **57-100 minutes a day**, but their health benefits are unproven ([Cochrane, Shrestha et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29926475/)). For his hip, the point of a break is mainly a **position change**, because sitting is a known aggravator. A sit-stand desk helps if he alternates every 30-45 minutes. Standing all day is not the goal. The app already gives the setup rule: seat height with hips at or above knees, and a walk every 30-45 minutes of sitting ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), lines 1053-1096).

|---|---|---|
| **1 min** "reset" | Stand, walk to refill water. 10 slow calf raises (Seated Bodyweight Calf Raise if he must stay seated). 5 standing glute squeezes with a gentle posterior pelvic tilt | catalog |

The 2-minute band option rests on one RCT: **2 minutes a day, 5 days a week**, of progressive elastic-tubing work cut neck/shoulder pain by about 40% in 10 weeks, as effectively as 12 minutes a day ([Andersen et al., University of Copenhagen](https://www.ucviden.dk/en/publications/effect-of-brief-daily-resistance-training-on-occupational-necksho/)). A secondary analysis also found fewer headaches ([OHS Online](https://ohsonline.com/articles/2011/06/12/brief-daily-resistance-training-eases-headaches.aspx)). The participants were mostly women who already had pain, and I could not confirm the exact exercise from the full text, so "lateral raise with tubing" is the reported version, not a verified one. Desk squats should go to a chair with hips staying above knees (Box Squat, pjf, if he wants a video), not to full depth. The 30-45 minute interval is an inference from metabolic studies. No trial has set the best break frequency for joint stiffness in adults over 35 ([desk research summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27208318/)).

## Tendons and strength need load two or three times a week, not daily

Some of what keeps a 40-year-old's lower legs and hamstrings healthy does not respond to daily light work. Calf-strain risk rises with **age and previous strain**, the two strongest risk factors ([Green & Pizzari 2017](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/51/16/1189.abstract)), and about **75% of calf injuries in English FA data involved the soleus** ([FMPA](https://www.fmpa.co.uk/app/uploads/2026/03/Under-the-surface.pdf)). The Achilles adapts only to high-strain loading at about 90% of maximal effort ([Arampatzis 2007](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/210/15/2743/17005/Adaptational-responses-of-the-human-Achilles)). It still adapts in older adults, but over about 14 weeks ([Epro/Karamanidis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5835978)). PJF reportedly calls tib raises "overrated" next to soleus and calf work ([Recapio summary of PJF ep. 058](https://recapio.com/digest/058-knee-health-myths-truth-about-tib-raises-stretching-and-more-pjf-podcast-by-pjfperformance)). Ben Patrick keeps them as strength work at about 2 × 15 ([Knee Ability series](https://kneesovertoesguy.substack.com/p/atg-knee-ability-series-3-of-10)). For a coach who stands and walks all day, soleus capacity is the priority, and tib raises are optional.

The app already runs a hip-strength plan on Monday, Wednesday and Friday that includes Copenhagens ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), lines 1701-1727). So this block goes on **Tuesday and Thursday, plus Saturday if he wants**, as a 10-12 minute add-on after the daily core. It should not add more side-hip work.

Two exercises wait until the hip has settled. **Eccentric Nordic Hamstring Curl (pjf)** runs at 2 × 3-5 once a week; low-volume Nordics still raise eccentric strength ([Cholp & Zemková 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1631205/full)), and programmes containing them roughly halved hamstring injuries ([van Dyk et al. 2019](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/early/2019/02/26/bjsports-2018-100045.long)). The pain sits at the back of the hip near the hamstring origin, so the PT should clear Nordics first. **Copenhagen maintenance** stays inside the PT sessions. ## Coaching days need a five-minute primer; desk days need the breaks

On **coaching days** his feet, calves and knees carry the load, and demonstrations are where the hip gets surprised. He should keep the morning core, using the 3-minute minimum version below if time is short. Then, before practice, he does a 4-5 minute primer. It starts with Sackmann's two warm-up openers, **Pogos** × 25 (trainheroic; skip them if landing pinches) and jumping jacks × 25 (not in the app yet). Then come **Backward Walk** 1-2 min (catalog; Patrick's entry-level knee tool, [SetForSet](https://www.setforset.com/blogs/news/joe-rogan-kneesovertoesguy-ben-patrick-how-to-fix-knee-pain)), **Glute Bridge** 1 × 10 (or standing glute squeezes if there is no floor), **Double Leg Functional Calf Raise (Optional)** 1 × 15, **Leg Swings** side-to-side 8/side in a small pain-free range, and **Band Pull-Aparts** 10. If his knees are cranky he adds **60 Degree Knee ISOs** 2 × 30 s, using the isometric's short-term pain relief before standing for hours. Alan Stein's sequence of pre-hab, then a dynamic warm-up ([PlaySports TV](https://www.playsportstv.com/basketball/warm-ups-drills)), fits this. HoopStudy's habit of following the physical prep with a short ball-skill block ([DJ Sackmann on X](https://x.com/DJSackmann/status/1878837426837147878)) suits a coach who demonstrates. During a flare he should not repeatedly demonstrate lunges, deep defensive stances or long low closeouts; a player can model them instead. After practice, lying down eases the hip, so 2 minutes of Posterior Pelvic Tilt Breathing or **Sidelying Grounding** (catalog; he should not lie on the sore side) makes a good cool-down.

On **desk-heavy days** the whole plan applies: the full A or B core in the morning, a break every 30-45 minutes, the 2-minute band dose once or twice, and one real walk. PJF's own "10 daily habits" episode makes the same case with movement snacks every hour and 10k steps ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/nz/podcast/the-pjf-podcast-elite-sports-performance/id1462156813); [PJF on X](https://x.com/pjfperformance?lang=en)). If the hip is grumpy by evening, the app's flare-friendly Pelvic Reset (Bench Hip Lift with Roller Squeeze, Sidelying Grounding, Posterior Pelvic Tilt Breathing, Supine Ball Squeeze Series) is the right extra, not more stretching ([hip_protocol.js](../src/lib/hip_protocol.js), lines 146-215).

## Sixty-six days to automatic: build it as a habit

HoopStudy's main contribution is its format, and the Day 10 recording shows what that format is: a **day-numbered, ~12-minute follow-along video** with many moves at short doses, flowing from standing to floor and back up. There are no long holds to dread and no decisions to make, because he presses play and follows. The routine above copies that shape on purpose, and the app should present it the same way: one continuous flow with a timer, not a checklist of separate exercises. HoopStudy's mobility products are about 10 minutes, daily, and sold on "the habit and accountability of prioritizing mobility" ([Playbook: Hoop Mobility](https://my.playbookapp.io/dj-sackmann/programs/hoop-mobility/627)), and Sackmann calls his own fixed morning routine "very important" because he travels so much ([Authority Magazine](https://medium.com/authority-magazine/how-dj-sackmann-thrives-as-an-athlete-turned-entrepreneur-521afa053d01)). Habit research supports that design. In Lally et al., 96 adults repeated a daily behaviour tied to a cue. Automaticity took a **median of 66 days** (range 18-254). The biggest gains came early, and **missing a single day did not materially hurt**, but very inconsistent people never formed the habit ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)). In office-worker trials, more participation meant bigger effects ([Chen 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6093121/)).

The practical rules follow from that. He should **pin the routine to one fixed cue**: straight after the morning coffee, on the floor beside the same mat. He should keep a **3-minute minimum version** for bad days, so the chain never breaks: Dead Bug 5 breaths, Glute Bridge 10, Band Pull-Aparts 15. He should track completion but treat a miss as "never miss twice", not a broken streak, and expect it to feel automatic somewhere around **two months**, not two weeks. ## Conclusion

For this coach, the daily routine is protection more than mobility work. Trials support three cheap, short things: frequent position changes, a couple of minutes of band shoulder work, and single-leg balance. All three fit around a hip that cannot take end-range stretching right now. The ambitious material from the basketball coaches (ATG split squats, couch stretches, 90/90 flows, plyometric tendon programmes) is either aimed at performance or aimed directly at his current aggravators. Sackmann's own Day 10 shows how much survives once those moves come out. Two-thirds of his routine is already hip-friendly. The other third can be swapped one-for-one without losing the follow-along rhythm that makes ten minutes a day stick.

For the app, the change goes beyond swapping exercises. It turns the mobility card from a static checklist into something that knows about the hip. It rotates, respects flare mode, offers a minimum version, and follows him into the workday, where most of his sitting happens. The right next step is to put the A/B list in front of his PT, record one or two more Sackmann days to check the swap table, and ship the rotation with the flare swaps switched on by default.
